For twelve years I've been asking the same question: why people buy what they
buy. For the first four I asked it in advertising, as an art director at
agencies in London and Madrid. For the next seven I asked it at a consumer
goods distributor, most of that time running one of its brands, where I moved
from creative insight to the kind you can measure.

I grew that brand from €8M to €12M in revenue, leading a team of 11 people with
eight reporting to me directly, and I set up its insights function myself. We
commissioned an outside research firm to test a pack design, and what they
found was that the brand had no value proposition. I took that finding to the
board instead of the one we'd asked for, and it set the agenda for the next two
years.

I worked with GfK panel data, Helium10, around 20,000 reviews (ours and our
competitors') and a survey of my own. With that I split the buyer persona into
two cohorts and opened a new category that reached 10% of revenue. The process
is written up as a seven-phase framework. Underneath it all: SQL and Oracle,
Python, Power BI, and a pipeline that takes 9,300 SAP materials to the PIM.

I now co-found Sapioverse and lead brand and strategy at Vertienza. I live in
Madrid and I'm open to relocating. The blog is in Spanish; the portfolio is in
both languages.
